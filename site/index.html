Food Delivery Reference Architecture

A single model of UK food delivery: who is involved, what software they run, what information it holds, how that information moves, and who holds power over whom. Everyone knows how a takeaway reaches their door, so you can judge what a model like this shows.

This is an illustration. The product names are real, but the flows are simplified and every value is invented. It describes no named company's actual systems, and it is not affiliated with any of them.

Open the map

Best on a laptop or larger screen: on a phone the map is very small.

How to use it

Zoom icon, at the bottom of a box
Opens that system or subsystem.
Card icon, at the top right of a box
Opens its details, including any schema and links. Press Esc to close them.
Clicking a line
Shows its relationship data, wording and any schema. Click the empty canvas to close it.
Clicking a box
Only focuses it and dims the rest. Press Esc to clear that.
Ctrl + K
Searches for any view or element by name.

A suggested route

  1. Landscape

    One map of who is involved and what software they run, with no vendor names yet.

  2. Delivery platform system

    The system is the role, and the products in it are replaceable. Uber Eats UK Limited is the business and Uber Eats is its software, so each can be described separately.

  3. Courier dispatch

    Use the card icon on Courier profiles to see its fields. The layers go down from map to system, subsystem, dataset and schema, all in one model.

  4. Follow one order

    Press Start, then Next, and read the notes. The same model can tell a story, with named people, one step at a time. Step 5 is the manual re-keying.

  5. Flows with no standard

    Click the red line, "staff re-key each order into the till", to see a proposed schema. The model finds the problems and holds a proposal for fixing them.

  6. Infrastructure

    Tablets on the counter next to a till that cannot talk to them: the same elements, this time showing where things physically sit.

  7. Who has power over whom

    Click a red line to see why, and its relationship data. A model can go beyond data flows to include soft power.

  8. Where the money goes

    Click a line to see its figures. These commercial relationships sit in the same model.

  9. Open questions and assumptions

    Use the card icon on Till system, then follow its link. What is not known is tracked against what it affects.
